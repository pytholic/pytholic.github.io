<blockquote class="epigraph">
<p>“I find it wholesome to be alone the greater part of the time. To be in company, even with the best, is soon wearisome and dissipating. I love to be alone. I never found the companion that was so companionable as solitude.”</p>
<cite>— Henry David Thoreau, <i>Walden</i></cite>
</blockquote>

Once I saw the dreary reality, I was drawn toward nihilism. Not the deleterious kind that the word usually suggests, just the realization that my life and all my actions have no intrinsic, objective, or cosmic purpose. A simple fact; neutral in its nature. However, nature as simple as it might appear, can have rather confounding psychological consequences.

Seeing it is the easy part. Thence, what follows is not. An absurd voyage into the abyss, from which there is no escape. The harder I struggled, the deeper I fell, as anything that crosses the event horizon of a black hole. Nature does not owe us any explanation; indifferent she is. I wandered for answers, some hint, the faintest sign, yet there was no resonance. As the physicist Joseph Polchinski once said, *‘Nobody ever promised you a rose garden.’*

I entered an unknown realm, where there was no guide. It frightened and enticed me in equal measure. The only way to learn is through experience; every idea is but a copy of some prior impression, and it takes a while before the impressions gather into any sense of the terrain. Yet the passage in this case is an ordeal — suffering that might never make any sense. The contortions, the wallowing, the soliloquys, the capricious days and nights. A dark, blind journey. And lonely. Should you, reader, be one of those unfortunate souls seeking some objective compass — I wish you better luck than mine.

<hr class="section-break">

People have much to say: the small talk, trifles, formalities, futile adulations, the sheer faith, the imposition, the certainty with which we propound and proceed; forever importuning those around us to elicit some sort of desired response or validation. I am not sure where this certainty comes from, for we are not Laplace’s Demon. We never are definitely right, we can only be sure we are wrong. For aught I know, no knowledge is absolutely certain. It is never too late to give up our prejudices. If history tells us one thing, it is that the human species is quite daft and inept.

I could no longer share the assumptions on which society rested, and the words lost their ground. Man is by nature a political animal, Aristotle wrote, and I was one. Yet I feel out of place, belonging nowhere. I lost interest in the mundane, and that is all there is to it. Whether I secluded myself or was ostracized, I ended up outside the polis. Life and I have yet to meet at a single point.

<blockquote class="epigraph mid">
<p>“I know of no better life purpose than to perish in attempting the great and the impossible”</p>
<cite>— Friedrich Nietzsche</cite>
</blockquote>

The fact of the matter is that the validation I once sought seems meager now, for I have found a new appetite. The allure of this bottomless abyss has drawn me so far that I no longer seek to escape or to solve, but rather to become one with it. Must we not assiduously chase and concern ourselves with the stardust, the mind, and the matter, with their origins, their behaviors, and their consequences? Must we keep asking about the food choice, remark upon the weather as though it were news, and enquire after a weekend or a health whose answer no one waits to hear, or the expression of our avaricious desires, each time we see someone? Such chatter abounds, and I began to execrate its prosaic and hollow nature.

<hr class="section-break">

I know there was scorn in those questions, supercilious, and I am not proud of it. Perhaps that was only how they said they cared, and I had ceased to hear it. My world has diverged too far too quickly; it is like living on a different plane. I have lost what I strove for my whole life: friends, family, camaraderie, beliefs, dreams, and purpose. My townsmen may see me as a loner, distant and aloof; I daresay it was no deliberate choice, for I have merely drifted apart. I confess, I do, at times, crave company, yet everything is loud. Forced away, as if by an electromagnetic force, I find my only solace in solitude, and in it the company of the dead, for those are the only ones I can resonate with.

<hr class="section-break">

Consequently, I began to seek peace: the tranquility, the quiet. In daily life, that meant seeking empty cafes, walking and exercising alone, taking rain checks, and averting every invitation. I thought that, alone, I would no longer have to deal with the noise. And for a while, it worked. No one asked what I had eaten, and I did not miss it. The silence was a clean room, and I the only soul in it. Thoreau, it seemed, had kept his promise. It appeared that I had found my refuge, except for an occasional faint murmur beneath the quiet.

<hr class="section-break">

But here is the kicker: the silence turned out to be even louder, like a dormant mountain that rumbles faintly beneath its calm, then erupts in all directions. My mind began to wander across places and realms, for I have no control over my thoughts. The monologue never ceases, and the more I seek the less I know. I am hungry; hungry for truth, but the truth is bleak and disillusioning. At this point, I cannot even reckon what “truth” means. In vain, then, I attempted to be secluded. It seems solitude was nothing but a mendacious attempt to escape the absurd.

It entails another cost, and the one I confess with least pride. Solitude is a fertile soil for sanctimony. With no one around to contradict me, my apparent clarity hardens into hauteur, and hauteur into a rectitude that looks down upon the faithful, the cheerful, the unburdened, and finds in every conversation another proof of its own verdict. The seeker, convinced that he alone is awake, comes to pity the sleepers. It feeds upon itself; each scorn begets the next, and detestation grows, leaving no room for empathy or understanding. And I, who marveled at the certainty of others, find myself propounding my own. If no knowledge is absolutely certain, mine is not either, and I have no warrant to look down. The lamp I carried into the dark casts a shadow of its own, and the shadow is called contempt.

I envy those who find it easy not to question their reality, be it faith or ignorance. I wonder what is worse: to have questions that cannot be answered, or to have answers that cannot be questioned? Pascal once said that all of humanity’s problems arise from our inability to sit quietly in a room alone, for we crave the thrill of the chase. I had my clean room, and I could not sit in it. I relate to this more with each passing day. Must we always seek more, and never rest in less? Siddhartha told his friend Govinda that to seek is to have a goal, and to find is to be free of one; the seeker, fixed upon what he seeks, can see nothing else. Perhaps that is why the answer recedes the faster I pursue it. To what end? And so, day and night, a soliloquy runs in the back of my mind, a cacophony that grows too loud. I seek a response, hoping it might end my chase or a part of it, knowing very well that one ending will lead to multiple new beginnings. Yet the universe is silent, for it was never listening, or perhaps it cannot.

<hr class="section-break">

‘The companion that was so companionable as solitude.’ I begin to suspect it is not solitude at all, but my own echo, rising from the burrows of the unconscious, where the voices of the dead still dwell. Perhaps this is why man is by nature a social animal. What I took for shallowness may have been a diversion, a way of not hearing one’s own echo. They fill the room with chatter and are spared it; I filled it with silence and was not. I will not pretend that noise is anything but noise, nor listen to it for its own sake. But I need not despise those who make it. I will keep my ear for the rare voice that is honest about what it does not know, and the rest I will bear. For my own part, I would keep candor in my speech and austerity in my manner, though not, I hope, condescension: to say plainly what I think, to claim no more certainty than I have, and to walk on, asking nothing of the road but that it continue.

<div class="annotations" markdown="1">

### Annotations

1. **Nihilism:** the view that life has no inherent, objective, or cosmic meaning or purpose.
2. **Event horizon:** the boundary around a black hole past which nothing, not even light, can escape.
3. **Joseph Polchinski:** an American theoretical physicist (1954–2018) known for his work in string theory.
4. **Impression and idea:** Hume's terms; an impression is a direct experience, and an idea is its fainter copy in memory or thought.
5. **Soliloquy:** speaking one's thoughts aloud to oneself, alone, as in drama.
6. **Laplace's Demon:** Laplace's imagined intellect that, knowing every particle's position and force, could predict the whole future with certainty.
7. **Polis:** the ancient Greek city-state; here, the community a person belongs to.
8. **Political animal:** Aristotle's phrase for humans as beings who flourish only in community (*Politics*, Book I).
9. **The absurd:** Camus's term for the clash between our need for meaning and a universe that offers none.
10. **The lamp:** my own lucidity, the clarity I carried into the dark, which also casts the shadow of contempt.
11. **Pascal's diversion:** Pascal's idea that people fill their lives with distraction to avoid facing themselves in quiet.
12. **Siddhartha and Govinda:** the seeker and his lifelong friend in Hermann Hesse's novel *Siddhartha* (1922).

</div>
