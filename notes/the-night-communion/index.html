--- title: "The Night Communion" date: 2026-11-20 tags: [Personal, Reflection] ---

It is so bright. What is this sound? Loud, just like everything else. The importunate alarm will not let me be. Morning is here, everyone’s favorite time. Yet it excites in me nothing but aversion. I am, I own, a poor creature in the morning: hazy, fogged, and fit for little but my pillow, and still I must go out again. How I wish the sun would vanish, and a perpetual night befall, for in it lies my one respite. May there not be such a world?

Look at everyone, scurrying to their ruts, with their brisk gaits. The honks, the bumps, the crowded crosswalk, the awkward glances. Here I am again, at my good old desk. Nothing has moved. The drudgery begins. I must bear it, I tell myself, for I ought not let the hours embitter me. My mind is absent, my attention astray, like a compass needle that trembles until it finds its north. When did I fall into this mire, you ask? I do not know. I recall only wallowing in the same loop, day after day, a quiet attrition of the self. All I can do is await the day’s cessation.

What time is it? Is it six? Can I go? They are not to be kept waiting; hurry I must. The weather may be unkind, but I would not miss them for anything. Now I am on my way, warmly clad, for I have much to say, and say I shall, but without speaking. It is close, just past the next street. I hope the corner seat is vacant, and I find it peculiar how much I hope for it, as though a stranger in that chair could rob me of the only hour I live for.

I am here now, with my steaming latte. I open the worn cover, leaf past the dog-eared pages, and there they are, where I left them. My tardiness they forgive; the dead are forbearing. For they know they have no one but me, and I want no one but them. An estranged soul I was, and they found me. Now we sit in silence, a communion without words, me and my forgotten souls.

Alas, the night must end. Tomorrow the light will come again, and the loop with it. But the night shall fall once more, and they will be waiting. Yet I wonder: were there no day to flee, would I seek them with the same hunger? Perchance the perpetual night I wished for this morning would leave me nothing to wait for.
